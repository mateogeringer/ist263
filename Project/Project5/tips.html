Tips for Beginners

Beginner boxers should focus on technique, stance, footwork, and defense

Technique

Understand how to throw a punch starting with your fist. When you punch it's important you have a tight fist, so you don't hurt your hand. Punches come from right beside your chin and return there when you land. There are 6 fundamental punches in boxing: jab, cross, left and right hook, and lead/rear uppercut. Yes there are variations of some like up jab, overhand right, or a check hook, but the 6 is what beginners should focus on learning. Punching isn't so much dependent on arm and upper body strength as people think, but more so the legs. Power comes from the legs and hips. Just like in many other sports that require you to throw or twist in some way you need to turn your hips and use your legs to generate power from the ground up.

Stance

Boxers either stand orthodox or southpaw. Orthodox are for people who are right handed so your right foot will be behind allowing your power right hooks/overhands or crosses to be from your right dominant hand. Southpaws stand the opposite with their left foot being behind with the right in front because their dominant hand is the left catering towards their power punches. A note to understand is some professional boxers like Terrance Crawford most notably can fight from either stance, which is very impressive. This allows him to switch stances when needed depending on his opponent and makes him a very dangerous fighter because you never know where he might throw from. His left and right hand punches are essentially almost equally as dangerous, but even Crawford has a preferable power hand they are just more equal.

Footwork

Footwork is one of the most underrated skills that a boxer can possess. With great footwork comes great understanding of distance and fully optimizing other skills like speed and power. For beginners it is important to focus on stepping in with your punches. My coach emphasized this when we are doing pad work by stomping his feet very hard while showing me a combination to help me understand where my feet should be and why. Moving your feet is hard for a lot of people at first so start slowly making sure you are working on not only stepping forward, but back and left/right. Whichever direction you are moving your foot closest to that direction always moves first. For example, if you step left your lead left foot will move to the left first followed by the right foot in that direction.

Defense

Obviously offensive punching isn't the only part of boxing, but defending incoming punches is equally if not more important. You probably heard the famous saying "Hit and don't get hit" which describes the ideal objective in boxing. Rolls, slips, parrying, blocking, ducking, and moving is how you defend yourself in boxing. Again start slow by incorporating these techniques into combos when shadowboxing, working on the bag, pad work, or light sparring. One boxer to look up to in regards to mastery of defense is definitely Floyd Mayweather Jr, who is known for his defensive masterclasses throughout his career that resulted in minimal damage and the ability to have a long lasting stay in the sport.

From my experience I found the most progress when I slowed things down doing drills over and over. Here are some of those drills:

  • Shadowbox a lot preferably with a mirror near you so you can see your technique. Step in/out when you punch, work on combos you enjoy incorporating defense as well after combos.
  • Use different bags in your gym — the standard heavy bag is great, but if your gym has a speed bag, cobra bag, double end bag, water bag, or any others make an effort to learn how to use them and practice. They all have different purposes and focus on different skills that a boxer will need.
  • Use the agility ladder and circle rings to work on footwork, there are hundreds of footwork drills online too that don't even require equipment that are great.
  • Make sure to work with a partner doing back and forth things where one person throws a combo and the other defends.
  • For an easy speed/endurance drill I like to use the standard heavy bag doing constant 1-2s repeatedly until you cannot. Rest a minute then repeat 5 times making sure to breathe and keep hands up.
