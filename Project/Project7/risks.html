Potential Risks/Benefits

Some benefits to think about before boxing:

Physical Health Benefits Mental Health Benefits
Great source of cardio Good outlet for stress
Trains muscles in whole body Learns discipline
Improves defensive capabilities Better self control and understanding dangers of fighting
Builds hand speed and power Great community and opportunity to meet others

While boxing has a lot of benefits, some things to consider are how you would like to train. Many people who decide to pick the sport up when a lot older — like 35+ — should train purely for cardio and the benefits listed above, not professionally, meaning no sparring and no amateur bouts.

Fighting and getting hit takes a toll on your body that, once you reach a certain age, isn't ideal to be dealing with. Fighting is a young man's sport, but training is for everyone.

If you are on the younger side — ideally under 25 — then you are young enough to really consider fighting amaturely and possibly working towards professional fighting.

If you do decide this is the path you want to take, here are some ways to reduce the risk of possible long term health problems:

How to Train Safely

  • Find a gym that has a good culture and coaches that prioritize the STUDENTS health — that means wearing head gear when sparring and not allowing newer people to jump into hard sparring with zero technique.
  • Take breaks and do not push through recurring head pain.
  • Communicate with coaches about injuries and how you feel daily.
  • Don't take unnecessary hard hits to the head to "build up toughness" — that isn't real.
  • Work on punching technique and defense — perfect the basics.

Answer these 5 questions honestly to find out where you stand before stepping in the gym.

1. How would you describe your current fitness level?

2. How do you handle getting hit or physical contact in sports?

3. How consistent are you with learning new skills?

4. Are you aware of the health risks involved in boxing?

5. What is your main goal for starting boxing?
