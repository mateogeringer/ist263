Tips for Beginners

Beginner boxers should focus on technique, stance, footwork, and defense

Starting boxing can feel overwhelming, but every great fighter started exactly where you are now. Click each section below to learn about the four fundamentals every beginner needs to master.

Click a section to expand it:

Understand how to throw a punch starting with your fist — keep it tight so you don't hurt your hand. Punches come from right beside your chin and return there when you land. There are 6 fundamental punches: jab, cross, left and right hook, and lead/rear uppercut. Power comes from the legs and hips, not just the arms. Turn your hips and use your legs to generate power from the ground up. Punching isn't so much dependent on arm and upper body strength as people think — the legs do the work.

Boxers either stand orthodox or southpaw. Orthodox is for right-handed people — your right foot stays behind, putting power behind your right hand. Southpaw is the opposite, with the left foot back and the right hand in front. Some elite fighters like Terrance Crawford can switch between both stances mid-fight, making them extremely dangerous and unpredictable. Start with whatever feels natural and master it before experimenting with switching.

Footwork is one of the most underrated skills a boxer can have. With great footwork comes great understanding of distance, which makes speed and power more effective. For beginners, focus on stepping in with your punches — my coach would stomp his feet very hard during pad work to emphasize where your feet should be on every combination. Whichever direction you move, the foot closest to that direction always moves first. Start slow and build the habit before trying to speed it up.

"Hit and don't get hit" — defending incoming punches is just as important as throwing them. Rolls, slips, parrying, blocking, and ducking are all tools in your defensive arsenal. Start slow, incorporating defense into shadowboxing and bag combos before trying it in sparring. Study Floyd Mayweather Jr. for the gold standard of defensive mastery — his ability to avoid damage is what gave him a long lasting career with minimal injuries throughout.

From my experience, the most progress comes from slowing things down and doing drills over and over. Here are some of those drills alongside resources to help you keep learning:

Drills to Practice

  • Shadowbox with a mirror nearby so you can watch and correct your own form. Step in and out with every punch, and incorporate defense at the end of each combo.
  • Use every bag in your gym — speed bag, cobra bag, double end bag, water bag. Each one develops different skills a boxer needs.
  • Use the agility ladder and ring circles to build footwork. There are hundreds of footwork drills online that require no equipment at all.
  • Work with a partner — one person throws a combo, the other defends. Rotate back and forth.
  • For a speed and endurance drill: throw constant 1-2s on the heavy bag until you can't continue. Rest one minute, repeat five times. Keep breathing and hands up throughout.

A Great Resource

One person who is an outstanding resource is Tony Jeffries. You can find his videos on YouTube and Instagram. He is an Olympic gold medalist who gives excellent tips — from how to properly wrap your hands all the way to full training programs. He's a fantastic resource for boxers at any skill level, and a genuinely funny guy as well.

Finally, just watch boxing content regularly — TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, anywhere. Seeing professionals and regular people box will naturally teach you what good and bad technique looks like. This sport is very hard, but also incredibly rewarding. Enjoy the journey!
